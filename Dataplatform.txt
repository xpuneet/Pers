The one-line proposition

The SIF and the data platform integrate only through two governed hand-off points — a push-in and a serve-out — with a shared canonical contract, Zero-Trust controls on every hop, and one named owner per hand-off. Everything inside the platform is theirs; everything in-flight is ours; nothing reaches across the line directly.

If they remember one thing, it's two doors, one contract, no reaching across.

Why this is the right answer (not just a preference)

It falls straight out of the principles rather than being invented:

Mediated & loosely coupled — no system, and no ADF pipeline, reaches into another's internals; they meet at a contract. That's the whole point of the SIF.
Canonical model as the contract — one semantics for operational and analytical worlds, so numbers don't drift and a change on one side is a translator change, not an estate-wide one.
Zero Trust — the platform is treated as a separate trust domain: private connectivity, identity on every call, encryption regardless of network, least privilege. We don't trust it because it's "inside Azure."
Clear ownership — a boundary with no owner is where incidents and blame live; every door gets a name.
The two integration patterns (the whole surface area)

1. Push in — SIF lands operational data, the platform ingests.
We publish operational data/events in canonical form to an agreed landing stage and raise an "available" event; the platform ingests from there (Snowpipe / streaming). Our responsibility ends at the landing stage; theirs begins there.

2. Serve out — SIF consumes curated data and exposes it.
The platform curates; we read it over Private Link through a thin serving layer and expose it to consumers via APIM with auth, caching and an SLA — marked analytical and eventually-consistent, never treated as a system of record.

Two hard rules that keep it clean:

Bulk/batch movement and all transformation stay inside the platform — the SIF only triggers, never becomes a bulk mover.
If a curated result must re-enter an operational system, it comes back through the SIF as a governed event/API — the warehouse never writes directly into a live system (informs, doesn't overwrite).
The Zero-Trust posture on both doors

Non-negotiables, applied identically inbound and outbound:

Private-only connectivity — Private Endpoints / Private Link to the stage and to Snowflake; no public paths; nothing traverses the internet between us.
Identity, not keys — every call authenticates with a Microsoft Entra managed identity, least-privilege RBAC; no account keys or secret-bearing connection strings; secrets (if ever unavoidable) are Key Vault references.
Encrypt everything, always — TLS on every hop even though it's private; encryption at rest, with customer-managed keys where classification demands.
Data protection at the line — PII classified and tokenised/minimised before it crosses the boundary; if the platform must match on a sensitive field it matches on a token we provide, not the raw value.
Default-deny & least privilege — the platform gets access only to the landing stage and the specific serving objects it needs, nothing wider.
Observability without leakage — correlation IDs and metadata in logs, not payloads, so the seam is traceable without spilling PII.
The ADF boundary (the bit they'll actually push on)

State it generously so it lands as protection, not a turf grab:

ADF is welcome as their platform-internal ELT tool — landing-to-curated inside Snowflake. It does not reach into our operational systems and it does not orchestrate cross-system flows. Where it needs operational data, it consumes what the SIF publishes, rather than pulling from sources directly.

That keeps their tool in their domain and stops a second, ungoverned integration platform emerging by accident.

What I'd ask them to agree in the room

Four things, each with an owner and a date:

The push-in path and trigger, and the serve-out path and trigger.
The landing stage and the serving/export location, each with one named owner.
Joint ownership of the canonical/data contract (including version and correction semantics) and of PII handling at the line.
The ADF boundary decision and an incident demarcation at the hand-off (a Snowpipe failure vs a push failure has a clear owner).
